## <b><u>Last Week's Wrap</u></b>

The week turned on a soft jobs report. US payrolls rose just 57,000 in June against a consensus near 110,000, with May revised down and the unemployment rate dipping to 4.2% only because participation fell to its lowest since 2021; the read took a September rate hike off the table, sent the two year yield down to 4.14%, and split the equity tape, with the Dow climbing 594 points on Thursday to a record 52,900 while the Nasdaq slipped 0.8% as semiconductors kept selling. It capped the best first half since 2021 for the Dow, with the S&P up 9.6%, the Nasdaq up 12.8% and the Russell 2000 up around 22% over the six months.

The semiconductor pain was sharpest in Asia, where Korea's Kospi fell 7.9% in a single session, SK Hynix dropped 14.6% and Samsung 9.1% as the memory trade unwound, and the Nikkei followed lower; the crosscurrent was that Asian growth data actually firmed, with Japan's services sector expanding and Chinese export orders at a roughly 20 month high. In single names, Nike beat on the quarter but the beat was flattered by a near billion dollar tariff refund, and with Greater China sales down 12% the shares fell after hours.

Europe had a cleaner week: the DAX rose 2.2%, the FTSE 100 1.7% and the CAC 40 1.65%, helped by softer euro area inflation that strengthened the case for the ECB to pause after its June hike. The dollar did the heavy lifting in currencies, falling about 0.5% for its worst week since April as the jobs data cooled hike bets, lifting the euro to 1.144 and the pound to 1.335, its best in three months, while the yen firmed back below 161 after brushing a fresh forty year low of 162.84 that revived intervention nerves.

Commodities stayed soft. Brent held near $72, close to pre conflict levels as Strait of Hormuz traffic kept recovering, and gold pushed toward $4,200 as lower yields and a weaker dollar did the work; over the weekend OPEC and its partners brought their meeting forward to Sunday and agreed a further phased output increase, a headwind for crude into this week. On the diplomacy, US and Iranian envoys held indirect talks in Doha around an Omani proposal for the strait, with both sides calling the tone constructive even as Tehran played down any direct meeting; the ceasefire holds, for now.

## <b><u>5 Points Looking Ahead to this Week</u></b>

1. **Rates: Fed speakers and the June minutes.** With a September hike now priced out and the market leaning toward no cuts at all in 2026, the June FOMC minutes due this week and a run of Fed speakers returning from the holiday are the test of whether officials still sound as hawkish as their dots or lean toward the soft labour data, with the two year at 4.14% the gauge.

2. **Commodities: OPEC's barrels meet a soft crude tape.** The weekend output increase lands with Brent already near pre war levels around $72, so watch whether the added supply and recovering Hormuz flows push crude into the high $60s, or whether any stall in the Doha talks puts a floor back under it.

3. **Equities: the semiconductor read sets the tone.** After Korea's 8% Kospi drop and another weak Nasdaq session, the AI and memory complex is the swing factor with Q2 earnings season a week away, so watch whether the group stabilises or the derating broadens beyond chips.

4. **FX: a softer dollar and a nervous yen.** The dollar sits at an eight week low near 101 with the euro at 1.144 and the pound at 1.335, so watch whether the dovish repricing extends the move or it steadies, and the yen near 161 where a slide back toward the 162 to 163 lows revives Japanese intervention risk.

5. **UK politics: the Labour leadership window opens.** Nominations open on 9 July with Andy Burnham the only declared candidate and more than 200 MPs behind him, so a coronation by mid July is the base case, and the read for gilts and sterling is his fiscal and spending stance, with cable already firm at 1.335 on the softer dollar.

## <b><u>Bull / Base / Bear: the week ahead</u></b>

**Bull (~35%).** The soft jobs read is taken as the start of a Fed pivot rather than a growth scare, the dollar keeps easing, the OPEC barrels cap oil and inflation fears, and the record breadth widens further as the Dow and small caps lead and even semis steady. Risk assets grind higher into earnings season.

**Base (~45%).** A consolidation: rates hold with a September hike off the table but no cuts priced, the dollar steadies near 101, oil drifts on the added OPEC supply, and equities digest a strong first half while the semiconductor complex stays the soft spot and the Fed minutes and speakers keep the range intact.

**Bear (~20%).** The 57,000 payrolls mark the front edge of a genuine labour slowdown, or the Doha talks stall and Hormuz flares again; the semis derating deepens after Korea's drop, the Dow's record fades, and a growth scare replaces the rate relief, with thin post holiday liquidity amplifying the move.

## <b><u>Focus idea of the week</u></b>

The cleanest expression of last week's split tape is to own the broadening rather than the index: a long in the equal weight S&P or the Russell 2000 against a short in the semiconductor heavy Nasdaq captures the rotation the soft jobs data set off, with rate sensitive and cyclical names leading while the AI complex derates. It is a defined relative value trade rather than a market call, and the main risk is a sharp semiconductor rebound that pulls the Nasdaq back in front, so size it modestly into thin post holiday liquidity.
