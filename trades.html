Live conviction = core 10 + technical analysis (0–2) + RSI check (0–1) = /13. The core (gap 3 + catalyst 2 + positioning 2 + confirmation 2 + stop 1) is struck at entry; the technical-analysis score (moving-average trend + Bollinger stretch + Fibonacci) and the RSI check are recomputed live every refresh and count toward the score shown on the badge, so a trade that goes technically offside loses conviction points in real time. The Derivatives Desk uses the same idea on its base /8 (→ live /11).
How we judge whether a trade is crowded

Positioning is the question: is the crowd already on the side we want to be on? If everyone is long what we are buying, there is no one left to buy and the unwind risk is high. We assess it from three reads, not an average of anything:

  • RSI on the absolute convention. RSI ≥ 70 = overbought (the buyers are exhausted); RSI ≤ 30 = oversold (the sellers are exhausted). We do not average RSI against a rolling mean — an oversold tape is oversold whatever its recent average was. An overbought reading on a long, or an oversold reading on a short, is flagged and costs a conviction point.
  • Moving-average stretch & Bollinger Bands. How far is price from its 50/100/200-day moving averages, and where does it sit in its Bollinger Bands? Price riding the upper band and stretched far above the 200-day means the move is extended and the late longs are crowded (fade-prone); hugging the lower band well below the MAs means the sellers are exhausted (squeeze-prone). A Bollinger squeeze (tight bands) says a crowded, low-vol consensus is about to break — we read these the same way for crowding as we read RSI.
  • Speculative positioning / flow. CFTC CoT net length for FX and commodities, fund-flow and options-skew reads for equities — where the money already sits and how stretched it is.
  • Pain trade. The direction that hurts the most people forces the biggest move; if our trade is also the pain trade for the consensus, positioning scores high.

The positioning component scores 0–2: 2 = the crowd is offside our trade (maximum fuel); 1 = neutral/mildly supportive; 0 = the crowd is already with us (high unwind risk).

How the universe is defined — what makes the cut

This is the broad, curated set of instruments the desk watches — the pool we then run the RSI screen (below) and the per-trade technicals against. It is not the whole market; it is built by applying these inclusion rules (the same way the Earnings tab uses its $10bn / US-Korea / sector filter), which is how we land on the 138 names:

  • Liquidity & tradability. Heavily-traded, optionable names with clean price action — so the technical signal is meaningful and you can actually act on it. "Heavily-traded" means a 20-day average dollar volume of ≳ $50m/day (most core names trade $200m–$5bn/day) and ≥ ~1m shares/day, so a desk-size clip fills without moving the tape. "Clean price action" means a continuous, gap-light 6-month daily history with a listed, two-sided options market (tight bid/ask, real open interest) — so the 50/100/200-day MAs, Bollinger Bands and RSI are computed on real prints, not thin or erratic ticks. No thin or erratic tickers.
  • Market cap ≥ ~$10bn for the core list; the AI supply-chain sleeve is allowed down to ~$2–3bn because that is where the optical / connectivity names (LITE, AXTI, CRDO, ALAB, SITM) sit.
  • Geography: US primary, plus major international via primary listings / US ADRs — Europe, UK, Japan, Korea, China.
  • Cross-asset, not just stocks. Single names across every sector plus liquid ETFs spanning equity, small-cap, rates (TLT), credit (HYG), gold/silver and EM/EAFE — so the screen flags extremes in bonds, credit and commodities too, not only equities.
  • Thematic overlay: an explicit AI build-out sleeve — semis, semicap, optical, connectivity, AI power/cooling, AI servers — because that is the dominant alpha theme right now.
  • Excluded: micro-caps, illiquid / niche single names, leveraged or inverse ETPs, and anything without a clean 6-month daily history.

How the JP Morgan GIS view feeds our house anchor

Every house view on the Portfolio page is consistency-locked to a macro anchor: the JP Morgan Global Investment Strategy (GIS) weekly cross-asset publication. GIS sets the top-down regime — Growth / Inflation / Rates / Positioning — that the desk uses as its starting grid. We read it as the institutional consensus on what is priced and what is not.

The lock works in three steps:

  1. Regime read. What does GIS say is the dominant macro factor this week? (Risk-on / Risk-off / Stagflation / Soft landing / Reflation.) We carry this into the morning brief as the regime field — visible in the top bar of every tab.
  2. Anchor alignment. Each house view must be consistent with the GIS regime. A LIKE on a rate-sensitive name with GIS flagging a rising rates risk premium is flagged with a yellow consistency note; a LIKE that contradicts GIS is blocked unless a specific, named catalyst overrides it.
  3. Conviction cap. If GIS signals crowded consensus or late-cycle risk, no house view scores above 6/10 on the conviction rubric regardless of fundamentals — the macro tail risk is treated as a hard cap on conviction.

GIS is a JP Morgan internal publication. References here are to the weekly summary theme; we do not reproduce the text. House view consistency notes cite the GIS theme as the anchor source.
