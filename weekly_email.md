## <b><u>Last Week's Wrap</u></b>

The week was a broad risk-on melt-up that a shock jobs report punctuated rather than derailed. All three US indices set record closes on Friday — the S&P 500 up 0.62% to 7,757.64, the Nasdaq up 1.30% to 26,690.62 and the Dow up 0.28% to 54,036.93 — capping the strongest week since April, with the S&P up about 3.6%, the Nasdaq up 5.2% and the Dow nearly 3%. The tape had already run hot: Monday saw the Dow jump roughly 900 points and the S&P close above 7,700 for the first time. Then Friday's July payrolls landed as a genuine surprise, the economy shedding 23,000 jobs against a consensus near +80,000, dragged by 53,000 lost government positions, while the unemployment rate slipped to 4.1% only because participation fell and average hourly earnings cooled to 3.2% year on year, the softest since May 2021. Crucially, in this hiking-bias cycle the read cut the odds of a September Fed hike rather than adding to cut bets, and equities took the growth wobble as rate relief.

Leadership was the mirror image of early July's semiconductor washout: this time the AI complex led from the front, with Nvidia up around 11% on the week ahead of its 26 August report, and the hyperscaler capex arms race supplied the fuel, as Alphabet lifted its 2026 capital-spending guide toward $195–205 billion and Amazon toward roughly $220 billion, underlining that the AI infrastructure build is still accelerating rather than rolling over. Risk appetite was broad enough that even SpaceX drew a strong secondary-market bid, up nearly a fifth on the week.

Bonds rallied with stocks. The two year yield fell to about 4.20% and the ten year to about 4.64%, each down roughly nine basis points on the week, as the soft labour data pushed back against the three FOMC dissenters who had pressed for a hike at the late-July meeting, where the Fed held its 3.50–3.75% range on a 9–3 vote. The dollar did the giving way: the DXY slipped toward 99.6, the euro pushed to a seven-week high of 1.1567, and the yen firmed to 157.70 after a rare joint Japan–US intervention earlier in the week steadied a slide back toward 159. Gold was the standout, surging about 2.4% on Friday to a record near $4,353 for its best week since January, as lower yields, a softer dollar and fading hike odds all pulled the same way.

Oil went the other way. Brent fell more than 7% on the week to around $86 as markets leaned into the prospect of an Iran–Oman understanding to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and return barrels to a tight market, the same diplomatic thread that has hung over crude since June now inching forward. Europe was mostly firmer — the DAX up about 1.8%, the CAC 40 up 2.4% and Italy's FTSE MIB up nearly 3% — with Britain's FTSE 100 the lone laggard, essentially flat. Asia was led by China, where the CSI 300 gained and July exports beat estimates in a spell of trade relief, while Japan's Nikkei was choppy but held near its highs and Hong Kong's Hang Seng lagged.

## <b><u>5 Points Looking Ahead to this Week</u></b>

1. **Inflation: July CPI on Wednesday is the whole ballgame.** With Friday's soft jobs print having cut September-hike odds, the 12 August CPI is the swing factor: a hot number — plausible given the Iran-driven energy shock that has kept costs elevated — revives the three dissenters' case for a 25bp September hike, while a cool print all but closes the door. J.P. Morgan had shifted to expecting a September hike before the jobs data landed, so CPI is the test of whether that call survives.

2. **Rates: a labour-versus-inflation standoff.** With the two year near 4.20%, the market is caught between a softening jobs market and sticky, energy-fed inflation. Watch the run of Fed speakers and the front end for whether the soft-jobs relief holds or the CPI print drags hike pricing back up toward the ~60% it carried into last week.

3. **Equities: can record breadth survive a hot CPI?** The best week since April was built on AI leadership and rate relief. A soft CPI extends the melt-up; a hot one tests whether the mega-cap tech bid can withstand a revived hike threat, with Q2 earnings winding down and Nvidia (26 August) still ahead as the next marquee catalyst.

4. **Commodities: oil's Hormuz discount against gold's record.** Brent near $86 is pricing progress on reopening Hormuz; any stall in the Iran–Oman talks puts a floor back under crude and, via energy-led inflation, back under hike odds. Gold at a record near $4,353 is the cleanest expression of the soft-jobs, weak-dollar read, so watch whether a hot CPI checks it.

5. **FX: a softer dollar and a steadier yen.** The DXY near 99.6 and the euro at a seven-week high of 1.1567 reflect the dovish repricing, while the yen at 157.70 sits just after intervention, so watch whether CPI reverses the dollar's slide and whether any drift back toward 159–160 revives Japanese intervention nerves.

## <b><u>Bull / Base / Bear: the week ahead</u></b>

**Bull (~35%).** July CPI comes in soft, confirming the September hike is off the table; the dollar keeps easing, AI leadership broadens the rally, and gold and equities both extend as a soft-landing-without-a-hike narrative takes hold. Records keep falling.

**Base (~45%).** A consolidation of a big week: CPI lands roughly in line, leaving a September hike a live but not base-case risk, the dollar steadies near 99–100, oil drifts on Hormuz hopes, and equities digest record highs while the market waits on more data and Nvidia.

**Bear (~20%).** CPI runs hot on the energy shock, the three dissenters' September-hike case roars back, yields and the dollar snap higher, and a record-chasing tape now leaning heavily on a handful of AI names gives back the week's gains in a hawkish repricing; or the Hormuz talks stall and oil reignites the inflation fear.

## <b><u>Focus idea of the week</u></b>

The sharpest read on the setup is that equities and gold are both pricing Friday's soft jobs report while a binary CPI sits two days out. The cleaner expression is to own gold as the direct beneficiary of the soft-labour, weak-dollar, fading-hike read, and to hedge — rather than chase — the equity melt-up into Wednesday, since the rally's narrow AI leadership is precisely what a hawkish CPI surprise would hit hardest. It is a defined, event-dated view on the Fed's dilemma rather than a market call; the main risk is a cool CPI that lets both a stretched gold and the momentum tape run further without you, so keep the hedge modest and dated to the print.
