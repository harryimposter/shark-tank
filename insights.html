The detailed map

A ceasefire is only as good as its last 24 hours, and this one's last 24 hours were strikes. The April truce between Iran and Israel cracked over the weekend in the worst exchange of fire in months; Iran announced a halt on Monday, and Israel answered it with an extensive strike on Iranian air defences and the Mahshahr petrochemical complex. Netanyahu has pointedly declined to call it a ceasefire; Iran says it will fire again if Israel keeps hitting Lebanon. So the screen you are reading is not pricing peace — it is pricing a truce that has already failed once and could fail again before the week is out.

Layer 1 — the regime. Two shocks, both still live: a war that keeps re-igniting and a CPI print landing this morning. Neither has resolved. The truce is unsigned and already broken once; the inflation number has not been seen. The map did not simplify this week — it got more dangerous, because the market spent a day pretending one tail was gone. The book that owns both tails is positioned correctly precisely because nothing is settled.

Layer 2 — the counter-intuitive hook. The most-quoted "good news" — Iran halting — produced a rally that lasted exactly one session. The hook is that the relief was rented, not owned: a blockaded Strait and a Netanyahu who won't sign mean the de-escalation has no anchor. Watch the oil price, not the VIX, for the real verdict — crude is pricing the physical fact (Hormuz shut), while equities priced a hope and took it back.

Layer 3 — the gap. Ground truth: the Strait of Hormuz remains under a dual US-Iran blockade, crude and gas shipments still disrupted, into a CPI print landing on still-elevated energy. What's priced: a failed chip bounce and a still-live year-end hike. The consensus narrative was "ceasefire, buy the dip" — and it just lost money. The gap is between a geopolitics that is still hot and an equity tape that wanted it cold.

The bear tail is fatter than it was: there are two ways to lose this week.

Layer 5 — priced vs not-priced. Mispriced the wrong way: any residual hope that Monday's halt was the end of it. Own the geopolitical convexity cheaply while the tape is still treating the war as yesterday's story.
