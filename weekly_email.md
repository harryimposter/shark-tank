LAST WEEK'S WRAP

Friday did not confirm Thursday's panic, and that is the first thing to correct from the pre market edition. The flash composite PMI printed 56.0, the fastest US business growth since April 2022, with services at 56.8, a twenty month high, hiring at its best pace in over a year and price pressures at ten month lows on services and six month lows on manufacturing. The tape bought it: the S&P 500 rose 0.43% to 7,674.37, the Dow added 517.80 points or 0.98% to 53,277.01, the Nasdaq Composite gained 113.29 to 26,180.45, the Russell 2000 rose 0.9% to 3,017.87 and the VIX fell back to 15.13. None of that rescued the week. The S&P still finished roughly 1.4% lower and the Nasdaq roughly 2% lower, so what Friday bought was one session, not the trend.

The long end is where the week was actually decided. The thirty year printed its highest yield since 2007 mid week — 5.31% on Monday, 5.34% on Tuesday on CNBC's print, a nineteen year high — on the largest monthly federal deficit since March 2021 and an inflation rate that has now sat above target for five straight years. Wednesday's response from Treasury was to double the maximum size of its long end buybacks from $2bn to $4bn per operation. That bought exactly one session. By Friday the thirty year had settled near 5.276%, up 3.9 basis points on the day, with the ten year around 4.74% and the two year at 4.24%. The detail that matters and keeps getting lost: the enlarged operations do not begin until 9 September. Nothing structural has changed yet, and the market has already looked through the announcement twice.

Where the relief liquidity actually went is the week's real signal. Bitcoin closed Friday up 6% at $76,943.90 after trading above $77,000, roughly 20% higher across the seventy two hours around the buyback, and gold reached $4,617.50, its best since mid May, up more than 2% on Friday alone. Both moved explicitly on US debt headlines, in the same week the national debt crossed $40 trillion. Note the correlation break: gold rallied WITH real yields backing up, not against them, which is not how it traded in July. A debt manager capping the price of its own liabilities while gold and bitcoin run is not a bond market being calmed. It is a sovereign being repriced, and the liquidity meant to settle the market that funds the government went instead to the two assets that have no sovereign counterparty at all.

Underneath the index the dispersion was violent and one directional. Big Tech fell more than 3% on the week and Meta around 7% across five sessions — the most rate sensitive, highest multiple names paid for the long end's move while the headline index recovered on Friday. The counterweight was Micron, which re rated toward $967 on a fiscal Q3 that did more than $41bn of revenue, up around 340% year on year, with EPS above $25 against $1.91 a year ago. The index level and the index leadership diverged all week, and it is the leadership that is telling the truth about the discount rate.

The weekend then added two trade shocks the Friday edition could not have carried. US Canada talks collapsed late Friday night and 50% tariffs took effect at midnight on roughly $20bn of Canadian goods, covering building materials, liquor, certain clothing and hockey sticks. Mark Carney called the tariffs a miscalculation, blamed what he described as uneconomic and unfair American demands, and set Canadian retaliation for 8 September on steel, dairy, agricultural equipment and pulp and paper. On the second front, the sixty day US Iran memorandum window expired on 17 August with no deal, Trump declared on 19 August what he called the most crushing economic operation ever taken against any country, and Bessent promised the toughest ever sanctions on Tehran. China answered by ordering its independent Shandong refiners to keep buying Iranian crude in open defiance. Brent posted a second consecutive weekly gain of about 6% to just under $94, with WTI around $87.

The crosses and the credit market close the picture, and they disagree with each other. The dollar index fell about 0.83% on the week to 98.82 on the fiscal story rather than on rate differentials, lifting the euro 0.98% to 1.1692, a three month high, the pound to 1.3641 and the yen to about 158.9, where Japanese July core inflation at 1.8% year on year firmed the case for a Bank of Japan hike in September. Europe closed Friday green — the DAX up 0.59% to 26,136.56, the FTSE 100 up 0.64% to 10,816.56, the CAC 40 up 0.37% to 8,484.43, the Bund at 3.26% — while the Nikkei fell 0.30% to 66,016.36 on yield sensitivity and Hong Kong and Korea firmed on better China demand signals. And then there is the market that did not move at all: US high yield spreads sat near 271 to 281 basis points, inside the richest decile of their own history against a long run median near 450, through a 2007 high in the long bond, a hawkish chair five days from the podium and two live tariff fronts.

5 POINTS LOOKING AHEAD TO THIS WEEK

1. Warsh's Jackson Hole debut lands on the same morning as core PCE, and that pairing is the week. The symposium runs 27-29 August on the theme of financial innovation; the new chair's first keynote is Friday morning, the same morning July core PCE prints, and nineteen days before the 16 September FOMC. He has said he would step back from near sighted debates and that the Fed is not constrained by market prices. With a September cut priced at zero and a hike near 30%, the surprise that repositions this market is hawkish, not dovish. The real tell is whether he engages with the long end at all — a chair who talks about fiscal dominance moves the thirty year far more than one who talks about September.

2. NVIDIA on Wednesday night is the entire equity tape. It reports after the close against consensus in the $92-94bn revenue range and $2.09-2.13 of EPS depending on the compiler, with Data Center estimated above $85bn, a four for four beat streak and roughly 95% implied odds of another. At that positioning the beat is required, not optional. The structural blind spot is that China data centre compute revenue is excluded from guidance entirely, which sits on exactly the fault line Beijing lit this week by defying US sanctions on Iranian oil. Salesforce, CrowdStrike, Synopsys, Marvell, HP and Dell report into the same window, so a disappointment does not stay in one name.

3. The thirty year against the 2007 peak, with the buyback still three weeks from starting. The long bond near 5.28% sits roughly sixteen basis points below the 5.44% pre crisis high, the doubled operations do not begin until 9 September, and the market has already looked through the announcement. A rebuild through 5.34% before the operations have even begun would be the market saying plainly that $4bn is not the marginal buyer, and it would put the question of who funds the deficit at the centre of the Jackson Hole week.

4. Credit is the market that has not moved, and it has the least room. High yield inside the richest decile of its own history has ignored everything the long end has done. Watch the two lines against each other: a thirty year through 5.44% without high yield widening in sympathy is the signal that credit is mispricing a genuine repricing event; spreads finally going through 300 basis points on the same catalyst is the signal the catch up is live. The gap is where this week's real risk is sitting, not in the index headline.

5. Oil is now a policy instrument, and the second tariff front opens the countdown. Brent near $94 after two 6% weekly gains is being driven by a sanctions campaign rather than by barrels, so the swing factor is enforcement against Iran's buyers, and China has already said it will not comply. Alongside it, watch a dollar at 98.8 that fell on the fiscal story rather than the rate differential, the yen into a live September Bank of Japan call, and the 8 September Canadian retaliation for whether the trade friction plateaus or escalates into the FOMC.

BULL / BASE / BEAR: THE WEEK AHEAD

Bull (~25%). Warsh keeps to structural themes and sounds untroubled by the long end, core PCE cooperates, and NVIDIA beats large enough to re-rate the complex rather than merely clear the bar. The thirty year pulls back from its 2007 test as term premium fears ease, the Canada and Iran threads plateau, and credit keeps its tights because growth really is as good as a 56.0 composite says. Equities recover the week's losses into month end and the debasement trade consolidates rather than extends.

Base (~45%). Nothing resolves. Warsh says little, by design and on the record, and core PCE prints close enough to consensus to settle nothing. NVIDIA beats by the usual 2-4% and the stock does not carry the index. The thirty year oscillates in a 5.15-5.35% band while the buyback is anticipated but not yet operating. High yield starts to lag modestly as the term premium story bites. Dispersion beats direction, the curve stays steep, and the long end sets the ceiling on the multiple.

Bear (~30%). Warsh delivers explicit hike optionality alongside a hot core PCE, the thirty year takes out 5.44%, and high yield finally snaps wider off its priced to perfection tights. China escalates its sanctions defiance into broader retaliation that also clouds the chip export detente and Wednesday's NVIDIA read, and the 8 September Canadian retaliation stops looking negotiable. The de-rating that began this week accelerates as the discount rate, not earnings, sets the multiple — and the dollar falls WITH yields rising, the combination that has no comfortable hedge except gold.

THE BOOK — HONEST SCOREBOARD

Three positions open, ten closed. MM-2026-013, short the US 2 year yield, was closed discretionarily on 23 August at -1.87% after 76 days: its catalyst was a dovish June FOMC repricing that resolved two months ago, and holding a rate cut expression into a zero cut, 30% hike setup was indefensible. The closed ledger now reads one win in ten. The SPX put spread hedge returned +28.57%; Broadcom, two Brent structures, the Hormuz call spread, the DAX/Nasdaq ratio, gold, short USD/JPY, long 10 year duration and now the 2 year all came off negative. A 10% hit rate and an average closed trade of -13.87% are the numbers, and the pattern in them has not changed: nearly every loss was a REACTIVE position sized for a geopolitical or Fed resolution the tape then refused to deliver on schedule.

The open book is still one position wearing three names. The 2s10s steepener opened on 1 June at +15bp is marked at +230.67% with the curve at 49.6bp. Short EUR/AUD is +1.01% and short EUR/USD is -0.66%, and neither has a live catalyst — EUR/USD's ECB event resolved in June, and EUR/AUD has had nothing driving it for weeks.

Here is the part worth sitting with. Last week's note flagged that the open positions shared one implicit assumption about the front end. That assumption has now broken twice. The 2 year trade is closed for it. And look at the steepener: it came IN from +246% to +230.67%, and 2s10s flattened from 51.9bp to 49.6bp, in the very week the long end printed a 2007 high. The flattening did not come from the long end rallying. It came from the front end selling off on Friday's PMI. So the same front end sensitivity that killed one position is now quietly taxing the one position carrying the entire book, and it is doing so in the exact macro environment the steepener was designed for. That is not a directional problem. It is a construction problem.

FOCUS IDEA OF THE WEEK

The idea follows directly from that construction problem, and it is a change of expression rather than a change of view. Shift the long end exposure from 2s10s to 2s30s. The thesis has always been fiscal supply and term premium, which live at the very long end; the ten year leg imports front end sensitivity that has now cost the book twice in a fortnight, once fatally. A 2s30s steepener isolates the part of the curve the thesis is actually about and stops the position being hostage to whether the next PMI lifts the two year. Take part of the +230% off in the process rather than defending the whole mark into two events that both act on the long end.

The second leg is the one regime expression the book does not own at all: protection on US high yield. Spreads in the richest decile of their own history, into a hawkish chair, a hot PMI, an NVIDIA print that has to clear 95% implied beat odds and two live tariff fronts, is poor asymmetry in one direction and very good in the other. The honest caveat is that this is a catch up trade with no catalyst of its own — if Warsh is data dependent and NVIDIA beats cleanly, spreads stay tight and carry bleeds it — so size it as dated event protection into Friday, not as a conviction short, and let 5.44% on the thirty year be the trigger to add rather than the level to chase. The outcome to design against remains a BEAR flattening, in which the July dissenters get their hike and the two year catches the thirty. That single scenario damages the steepener and the short EUR/USD leg together, which is precisely what happened in miniature this week, twice, as a warning.
