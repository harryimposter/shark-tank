## <b><u>Last Week's Wrap</u></b>

The week belonged to the long end of the Treasury curve. The 30 year yield hit 5.31% on 17 August, its highest since July 2007 and within about thirteen basis points of the pre crisis peak, on heavy long dated issuance, sticky tariff fed inflation and a market recalibrating around new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh ahead of his first Jackson Hole keynote. Washington tried to fix it: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent doubled the size of the long dated buyback operations from $2bn to at least $4bn apiece. The bond market gave it exactly one day. Yields resumed climbing, the Dow fell roughly 700 points on Wednesday as the plan failed to reassure, and the 30 year still settled the week near 5.28% with the 10 year around 4.74% and the two year at 4.24%, leaving 2s10s about 50 basis points steep.

What the relief liquidity did instead was light the debasement trade. In the seventy two hours around the buyback, bitcoin ripped roughly 20% from the mid $60,000s to close Friday up 6% near $76,900 after trading above $77,000, and gold broke to its best level since mid May at about $4,617, up more than 2% on Friday alone. Both moved explicitly on US debt headlines, in the same week the national debt crossed $40 trillion. The two assets with no sovereign counterparty repriced Washington's balance sheet faster than the market that actually funds it, and gold did it while real yields were backing up rather than falling, which is the correlation break worth marking.

Equities finished lower on the week despite a strong Friday. The S&P 500 fell about 1.4% and the Nasdaq about 2%, with the Dow down too, before Friday's bounce on a blazing flash composite PMI of 56.0, the fastest US business growth since April 2022, with services at a twenty month high, hiring at its best pace in over a year and price pressures at multi month lows. That print lifted the S&P 0.4% to 7,674.37, the Dow 1.0% to 53,277.01, the Nasdaq Composite 0.4% to 26,180.45 and the Russell 2000 0.9% to 3,017.87, and knocked the VIX back to 15.14. The tell is underneath the index: Big Tech fell more than 3% on the week and Meta around 7% in five sessions, so the most rate sensitive, highest multiple names were the ones actually paying for the long end's move even as the headline recovered. Micron was the offsetting single name, re rating toward $967 on a fiscal Q3 that did more than $41bn of revenue, up around 340% year on year.

Geopolitics ran two live fronts. The sixty day US Iran memorandum window expired on 17 August with no deal, Trump declared what he called the most crushing economic operation ever taken against any country on 19 August, and Bessent promised the toughest ever sanctions on Tehran; China answered by ordering its independent Shandong refiners to keep buying Iranian crude in open defiance. Brent responded with a second consecutive weekly gain of about 6% to just under $94, with WTI around $87. Then, late Friday night, US Canada trade talks collapsed and 50% tariffs took effect at midnight on roughly $20bn of Canadian goods, from building materials and liquor to hockey sticks. Mark Carney called the tariffs a miscalculation, blamed uneconomic and unfair American demands, and set Canadian retaliation on steel, dairy, agricultural equipment and pulp and paper for 8 September.

Currencies went the other way from the yields, which is the point. The dollar index fell about 0.8% on the week to 98.82 on fiscal outlook concern and the buyback news, lifting the euro 1% to $1.168, its best in three months, the pound to 1.3641 and the yen to about 158.9, where Japanese July core inflation at 1.8% year on year firmed the case for a Bank of Japan hike in September. Europe closed Friday green, with the DAX up 0.59% to 26,136.56, the FTSE 100 up 0.64% to 10,816.56 and the CAC 40 up 0.37% to 8,484.43, and the Bund at 3.26%; Asia was mixed, the Nikkei down 0.30% to 66,016.36 on yield sensitivity while Hong Kong and Korea firmed on better China demand signals. The one market that did not move at all was credit: US high yield spreads sat near 271 to 281 basis points, inside the richest decile of their own history against a long run median near 450.

## <b><u>5 Points Looking Ahead to this Week</u></b>

1. **Rates: Warsh's Jackson Hole debut lands on the same morning as core PCE.** The symposium runs Thursday and Friday on the theme of financial innovation and payments, and the new Chair gives his first keynote as Chair on Friday 28 August, the same morning July core PCE prints. That pairing is the single highest risk event of the week for a sharp two sided move in rates, the dollar and equities, with CME pricing zero odds of a September cut, roughly 30% odds of a hike and Warsh already on record that he is not constrained by market prices.

2. **The long end versus the 2007 peak.** The 30 year at 5.28% sits about sixteen basis points below the 5.44% pre crisis high, with the doubled buyback already spent and no obvious second lever. Watch whether a hawkish keynote pushes the long bond through that line, because a term premium driven break is a very different signal from the growth driven backup Friday's PMI would justify, and the 2s30s slope is the cleanest read on which one it is.

3. **Equities: Nvidia on Wednesday is priced for perfection.** The company reports after the close on 26 August having guided to about $91bn of revenue plus or minus 2%, against consensus near $92bn and $2.09 of EPS with Data Center estimated above $85bn, a four for four beat streak and roughly 95% implied odds of another. The blind spot is that China data centre compute revenue is excluded from guidance entirely, on the same fault line as Beijing's open defiance over Iranian oil, and Marvell, Salesforce, CrowdStrike, Synopsys and Dell fill in the rest of the AI capex read.

4. **Commodities: an oil price that is now a policy instrument.** Brent near $94 after two 6% weekly gains is being driven by a sanctions campaign rather than by barrels, so the swing factor is enforcement against Iran's buyers rather than supply and demand, and China has already said it will not comply. A tightening that bites lifts crude further into a Fed that cannot ease against it; any sign of a negotiated off ramp takes the cost push out of the September inflation arithmetic quickly.

5. **Credit, the dollar and the second tariff front.** High yield at the richest decile of its history has ignored a 2007 high in the long bond, a hawkish Chair five days from the podium and two simultaneous trade shocks, which makes it the market with the least room and the most catching up to do. Alongside it, watch a dollar at 98.8 that fell on the fiscal story rather than on rate differentials, the yen into a live September Bank of Japan call, and the countdown to Canadian retaliation on 8 September for whether the trade friction plateaus or escalates into the FOMC.

## <b><u>Bull / Base / Bear: the week ahead</u></b>

**Bull (~30%).** Warsh lands data dependent rather than overtly hawkish, core PCE cooperates, and the 30 year backs away from its 2007 test as term premium fears ease. Nvidia beats and guides the Data Center line high enough on Wednesday to confirm the capex cycle, the Canada and Iran threads plateau rather than escalate, and credit keeps its tights because growth really is as good as a 56.0 composite says. Risk assets rally, the curve holds its steepness on a lower long end, and gold and bitcoin consolidate their gains rather than give them back.

**Base (~45%).** Warsh reasserts inflation fighting credibility without an explicit hike signal, and the 30 year ranges just below 5.31% into the September FOMC while core PCE prints close enough to expectations to settle nothing. Nvidia beats but the print reads as good rather than great given the China exclusion, and the stock digests instead of melting up. High yield begins to lag modestly as the term premium story bites, equities stay choppy with leadership still narrow and rate sensitive names still soft, and gold and bitcoin hold their bid.

**Bear (~25%).** Warsh delivers explicit hike optionality alongside a hot core PCE, the 30 year takes out the 5.44% peak, and high yield finally snaps wider off its priced to perfection tights. China escalates the Iran sanctions defiance into broader retaliation that also clouds the chip export detente and Wednesday's Nvidia read, Canadian retaliation on 8 September stops looking negotiable, and Brent runs through $95 into a Fed that cannot cut. Risk falls hard while gold and bitcoin extend, which is the stagflation adjacent tape rather than an ordinary drawdown.

## <b><u>Focus idea of the week</u></b>

The cleanest expression of last week is to own the gap between the two markets that disagreed, which means buying protection on US high yield credit against the long end's move rather than chasing either leg outright. Spreads near 271 to 281 basis points are inside the richest decile of their own history at a moment when the 30 year is at a 2007 high, a hawkish Chair speaks in five days, Nvidia has to clear a 95% implied beat, and two tariff fronts are live, so the asymmetry is poor in one direction and very good in the other. The rates twin of the same view is a 2s30s steepener, which isolates the long end specific term premium story rather than betting on the direction of policy, and it is the more liquid way to hold the idea through Friday's event risk. The honest risk is that this is a catch up trade with no catalyst of its own: if Warsh is data dependent and Nvidia beats cleanly, credit stays tight and carry bleeds the position, so size it as protection into a dated event rather than as a conviction short, and let the 5.44% line on the 30 year be the trigger to add rather than the level to chase.
